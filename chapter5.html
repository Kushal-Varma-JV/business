1Concept

Organising = process that starts putting plans into action by clarifying jobs & working relationships and deploying resources to achieve goals. Theo Haimann: "Process of defining and grouping the activities of the enterprise and establishing authority relationships among them."
🔄 RoleMeans of translating plans into action
🏗️ OutputCreates the organisation structure (roles + relationships)
📌 School fete → work split into food, decoration & ticketing committees under one in-charge = organising
🔑 Organising brings order out of chaos & creates an environment for teamwork

2Process (4 Steps)

🧠 I Don't Ask Everyone → Identify · Departmentalise · Assign · Establish
1. Identify & divide work
Split work (as per plans) into manageable activities → avoids duplication, shares burden
2. Departmentalisation
Group similar activities into departments (basis: territory, product) → specialisation
3. Assign duties
Each dept gets a head; jobs given as per skills (right job ↔ right person)
4. Establish authority & reporting
Who takes orders from whom, who reports to whom → hierarchy + coordination
📌 12 students unloading library books → chaos if each works their own way; grouping + quotas + reporting = faster & better

3Importance (7)

🧠 Smart Cats Only Adopt Easy Daily Exercise
  • Specialisation — repeating a specific job → experience, productivity ↑, workload ↓
  • Clarity in working relationships — who reports to whom; fixes responsibility & extent of authority
  • Optimum use of resources — no overlapping/duplication → less wastage of material, money, people
  • Adaptation to change — structure can be modified → smooth transition + stability
  • Effective administration — clear job descriptions → no confusion, easy management
  • Development of personnel — delegation frees managers to innovate; subordinates realise potential
  • Expansion & growth — add jobs, depts, product lines, new territories → customers, sales, profit ↑

4Organisation Structure

Framework within which managerial & operating tasks are performed. Outcome of the organising process. Shows relationships between people, work & resources. Shown in an organisation chart.
👥 Span of ManagementNo. of subordinates a superior can effectively manage → determines the LEVELS of management
📈 When neededWhenever the firm grows in size or complexity
✅ EnsuresSmooth flow of communication + better control
🔑 Peter Drucker: wrong structure can seriously impair business performance & even destroy it
2 types → Functional · Divisional

5Functional vs Divisional

🏭 Functional Structure

Grouping similar/related jobs into major functions (production, marketing, finance, HR…), each a separate dept. All report to a coordinating head.
Managing Director
HRMarketingR&DPurchasing
✅ Some Cows Eat Dry Tasty Apples (6)
  • Specialisation (occupational) — similar tasks → efficient use of manpower
  • Control & coordination within a dept — tasks are similar
  • Efficiency (managerial & operational) → more profit
  • Duplication minimal → economies of scale, lower cost
  • Training easier — only a limited range of skills
  • Attention — every function gets due attention
❌ Empires Can Crumble Instantly (4)
  • Functional Empires — dept goals put above organisation's goals
  • Coordination problems — info must cross many separate depts
  • Conflict of interests — e.g. sales wants customer-friendly design, production struggles
  • Inflexibility — narrow outlook; heads not trained for top posts
🎯 Suitable: large organisation, diversified activities, high specialisation needed
📌 Registered office Delhi, factory Gurgaon, sales Faridabad (consumer products) → Functional

🧩 Divisional Structure

Separate business units (divisions) formed on product lines. Each division is multifunctional, self-contained & a profit centre under a divisional manager.

  • Expansion easy — add a new division without disturbing existing ones

6Formal vs Informal

📋 Formal Organisation

Structure deliberately designed by management to accomplish a task; clear boundaries of authority & responsibility. Chester Barnard: "A system of consciously coordinated activities of two or more persons toward a common objective." Can be functional or divisional.
🔍 Features: Real Managers Can Design Work (5)
  • Relationships specified — who reports to whom
  • Means to achieve objectives — lays down rules & procedures
  • Coordinates efforts — links & integrates departments
  • Deliberately designed — by top management
  • Work emphasised — over interpersonal relationships
✅ Fine Roles Unite Every Staff (5)
  • Fixing responsibility easy — relationships clearly defined
  • Role clarity — duties specified → no ambiguity, no duplication
  • Unity of command — via established chain of command
  • Effective goal accomplishment — framework + everyone knows their role
  • Stability — behaviour predictable due to rules
❌ Delays Crush Learning (3)
  • Delays (procedural) — chain of command must be followed → slow decisions
  • Creativity ignored — no deviation from rigid policies
  • Limited picture — ignores human relationships; doesn't show how org really works

☕ Informal Organisation

Network of social relationships among employees arising from interaction at work. Keith Davis: "Network of personal and social relations… arising spontaneously as people associate with one another."
📌 Sunday cricket group · cafeteria coffee group · dramatics group
🔍 Features: Ordinary Norms Can Spread Silently (5)
  • Originates within formal org — from personal interaction
  • Norms — behaviour set by group norms, not official rules
  • Channels of communication — independent, no fixed direction
  • Spontaneous — not deliberately created by management
  • Structureless — no definite form; complex social network
✅ Friends Share Fun (3)
  • Faster communication — no prescribed lines → quick spread & feedback
  • Social needs met — like-minded people → belongingness, job satisfaction
  • Fills gaps of formal org — e.g. test employees' reaction to plans
❌ Really Rude People (3)
  • Rumours — becomes a destructive force
  • Resistance to change — may delay or restrict growth
  • Pressure to conform — harmful if group norms oppose org interests
🔑 Can't be eliminated → management should recognise it & use BOTH formal and informal together
📌 Rigid formal sewing-machine company (delays, red tape, high turnover) → encourage informal organisation alongside formal

7Delegation

Delegation = downward transfer of authority from superior to subordinate. Theo Haimann: "Granting of authority to subordinates to operate within prescribed limits."
🤔 WhyNo manager can do everything; frees time for high-priority work
⚠️ Not abdicationManager stays fully accountable; authority can be taken back

Elements (3)

🧠 D-A-I → Authority is Delegated · Responsibility is Assumed · Accountability is Imposed
  • Authority — right to command & take decisions; arises from scalar chain; highest at top; limited by laws & org rules
  • Responsibility — obligation to properly perform assigned duty; arises from superior–subordinate relationship
  • Accountability — answerability for final outcome; enforced through regular feedback

MeaningRight to commandObligation to performAnswerability for outcome

Authority > Responsibility= misuse of authority
Responsibility > Authority= person becomes ineffective
🔑 Authority must be commensurate (equal) with responsibility
📌 Foreman given 200-unit target but no authority to get tools → can't be blamed (authority ≠ responsibility)

Importance (6)

🧠 Every Elephant May Grow Huge Calmly
  • Effective management — managers freed from routine → focus on important matters
  • Employee development — use talent, gain skills → better future managers
  • Motivation — trust shown → self-esteem & confidence ↑
  • Growth facilitated — trained staff ready to lead new ventures/branches
  • Hierarchy basis — creates superior–subordinate relationships
  • Coordination better — clear powers, duties, answerability → no overlap

8Decentralisation

Decentralisation = delegation of authority to ALL levels; decision-making pushed down to the points of action. Louis Allen: "Systematic effort to delegate to the lowest level all authority except that which can be exercised at central points."
🏛️ CentralisedDecision-making authority kept at top
🌐 DecentralisedAuthority shared with lower levels
🔑 Relative terms → no organisation is fully either (full centralisation = no need for hierarchy; full decentralisation = no need for top managers). Every organisation has a balance of both
📈 As a firm grows → tends to decentralise (people close to operations know more)
💡 Philosophy → people are competent, capable & resourceful; authority dispersed selectively

Importance (6)

🧠 I Think Quick Responses Give Control
  • Initiative develops — self-reliance & confidence; spots future leaders
  • Talent for future — reservoir of managers for promotion
  • Quick decisions — made near point of action; less distortion of info
  • Relief to top management — less supervision → time for policy decisions
  • Growth facilitated — competition between depts → productivity & returns ↑
  • Control better — each level evaluated (balanced scorecard, MIS)
⚠️ Caution → can cause disintegration; keep major policy decisions centralised
📌 Wipro (centralised → decentralised) · J&J (run as 190 smaller companies) · X Ltd FMCG losing share to MNCs → Decentralise

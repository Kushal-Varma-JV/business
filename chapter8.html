1Concept

Controlling = ensuring that activities in an organisation are performed as per the plans, and resources are used effectively & efficiently to achieve goals. Koontz & O'Donnell: "Managerial control implies the measurement of accomplishment against the standard and the correction of deviations to assure attainment of objectives according to plans."
📍 TracksKeeps a check on the progress of activities
✅ EnsuresActivities match standards set in advance → goals achieved

Features (4)

🧠 Good Pianists Practise Constantly
  • Goal-oriented — ensures resources are used to achieve predetermined goals
  • Pervasive — all levels (top, middle, lower) + all organisations (schools, military, hospitals, clubs, businesses)
  • Primary function — of every manager
  • Continuous — NOT the last function; brings the management cycle back to planning
🔑 Controlling completes one cycle & improves planning in the next (finds deviations → analyses causes → corrects → better future plans)
📌 Departure Control System (airlines): automates check-in, boarding pass, baggage, boarding load & aircraft checks → manage situations & correct before damage is done
📌 Mr Shantanu: constant & continuous check on all activities + all employees' performance so targets are met → Goal-oriented · Continuous · Pervasive

2Importance (6)

Control is an indispensable function. Without it, the best of plans can go awry.
🧠 All Juniors Eat Eggs On Cake
  • Accomplishing organisational goals — measures progress, brings deviations to light, indicates corrective action → keeps org on the right track
  • Judging accuracy of standards — checks if standards are accurate & objective; reviews & revises them as the org & environment change
  • Efficient use of resources — reduces wastage & spoilage; every activity done as per standards & norms
  • Employee motivation — staff know in advance what is expected & how they'll be appraised → better performance
  • Order & discipline — close check on activities → minimises dishonest behaviour
  • Coordination in action — every dept & employee follows predetermined standards that are coordinated with each other
📌 NY import-export firm logs suspects' keystrokes → 2 employees caught embezzling $3 million = Order & discipline
📌 M Ltd: missed sales & customer-satisfaction targets, new rivals with better tech & pricing → benefits of good control = the 6 points above

3Limitations (4)

🧠 Don't Leave Rice Cold
  • Difficulty in setting quantitative standards — morale, job satisfaction & human behaviour are hard to measure → control loses effectiveness
  • Little control on external factors — govt policies, technological changes, competition
  • Resistance from employees — seen as a restriction on freedom (e.g. objecting to CCTV watch)
  • Costly affair — expenditure, time & effort; small firms can't afford it; cost must not exceed benefits
📌 When does a control system lose effectiveness? → when standards can't be defined in quantitative terms

4Planning & Controlling

Planning & controlling are inseparable twins of management. Planning initiates the management process; controlling completes it.
📋 Planning without controlling= Meaningless
🙈 Controlling without planning= Blind (no standards → nothing to control)
🔑 Planning is a prerequisite for controlling → it provides the standards control measures against

💬 "Planning looks ahead, controlling looks back": comment

✅ True becausePlans are for the future, based on forecasts · Control is a postmortem of past activities to find deviations
❌ But alsoPlanning is guided by past experience · Corrective action aims to improve future performance
◐ Only PARTIALLY correct: both are backward-looking AND forward-looking

They reinforce each other (2)

  • 1. Planning based on facts → makes controlling easier & effective
  • 2. Controlling → improves future planning with info from past experience
📌 M Ltd: relate planning with control → set clear sales & satisfaction targets (standards) → monitor & compare → feed results back to revise plans for new competition

5Controlling Process (5 Steps)

🧠 Some Monkeys Can Actually Cook
1. Setting performance standards
Criteria/benchmarks to measure actual performance against
2. Measuring actual performance
Objective & reliable; same units as standards
3. Comparing actual with standards
Reveals the deviation between actual & desired results
4. Analysing deviations
Set acceptable range; find exact causes; use critical point control & MBE
5. Corrective action
Only when deviation exceeds acceptable range, so it doesn't recur
↻ Step 5 feeds back into planning → the cycle starts again
📌 Deviation → term for the difference between standard & actual performance

6Standards, Measurement & Comparison

🎯 Step 1: Standards

🔢 QuantitativeCost to be incurred, revenue to be earned, units produced & sold, time per task
💭 QualitativeImproving goodwill, employee motivation level
📐 Best practiceSet precise quantitative standards → easier comparison (defects 10 → 5 per 1,000 by quarter end). Define qualitative ones in measurable terms
🔄 FlexibleModify standards as the internal & external environment changes to keep them realistic
📌 Self-service fast food: customer satisfaction (qualitative) → measured as time to get a table, place the order & collect it

Standards by Functional Area

AreaStandards
🏭 ProductionQuantity · Quality · Cost · Individual job performance
📣 MarketingSales volume · Sales expense · Advertising expenditure · Individual salesperson's performance
👥 HRMLabour relations · Labour turnover · Labour absenteeism
💰 Finance & AccountingCapital expenditure · Inventories · Flow of capital · Liquidity

📏 Step 2: Measurement

🧠 Techniques: Please Stop Pushing → Personal observation · Sample checking · Performance reports
  • When — during performance where possible (check each part before assembly; continuously monitor gas particles in the air for safety)
  • Employee — performance report by superior
  • Company — ratios at intervals: gross profit ratio, net profit ratio, ROI
  • Marketing — units sold, increase in market share
  • Production — pieces produced, defective pieces per batch
🏠 Small organisationEvery piece checked for quality
🏢 Large organisationSample checking: random pieces checked

⚖️ Step 3: Comparison

Reveals deviation · easiest with quantitative standards → worker's units per week vs standard weekly output

7Deviations & Corrective Action

🔑 Some deviation is expected in all activities → decide the acceptable range; key areas get urgent attention

🎯 Critical Point Control

Focus control on Key Result Areas (KRAs), the points critical to success. Checking every activity is neither economical nor easy. If a critical point goes wrong, the whole organisation suffers.
📌 Manufacturing: 5% rise in labour cost is more troublesome than 15% rise in postal charges

🚨 Management by Exception

Only significant deviations beyond the permissible limit are brought to management's notice. Also called control by exception. Belief: "An attempt to control everything results in controlling nothing."
Within 2% labour cost riseAcceptable: not reported
Major (say 5%)Immediate action on priority basis
✅ Advantages (4): Sharks Find Deep Islands
  • Saves time & effort — managers handle only significant deviations
  • Focus on important areas — better use of managerial talent
  • Delegation & morale — routine problems left to subordinates
  • Identifies critical problems — that need timely action
📌 Writewell: Bhanu Prasad 10 units short for 2 days; Arfaaz wants him fired → Management by Exception: minor deviation, shouldn't reach the CEO; no termination

🔍 Causes of Deviations (6)

🧠 Uncle Dave Is So Overly Excited
  • Unrealistic standards
  • Defective process
  • Inadequacy of resources
  • Structural drawbacks
  • Organisational constraints
  • Environmental factors — beyond the organisation's control
🔑 Find the EXACT cause → otherwise correct action isn't possible; report & act at the appropriate level

🛠️ Step 5: Corrective Action

  • Within limits — no action needed
  • Production target missed — train employees
  • Project behind schedule — extra workers & equipment + overtime
  • Can't be corrected — revise the standards

8Techniques of Control

📝 Named only in the book's summary & exercises; not explained in the text → check with your teacher if they're in your syllabus
Techniques
🕰️ Traditional (4)🚀 Modern (6)

🕰️ Traditional (4)

🧠 Penguins Slide By Boats
  • Personal observation — first-hand info, psychological pressure to perform ✗ time-consuming
  • Statistical reports — averages, percentages, ratios, charts → easy comparison
  • Breakeven analysis — volume where total revenue = total cost (no profit, no loss); BEP = Fixed cost ÷ (Price − Variable cost per unit)
  • Budgetary control — budgets act as standards; compare actual vs budget & correct

🚀 Modern (6)

🧠 Rabbits Run Rapidly; Monkeys Play Musically
  • Return on Investment — net income ÷ total investment; checks efficient use of capital
  • Ratio analysis — liquidity, solvency, profitability, turnover ratios
  • Responsibility accounting — divisions as centres (cost, revenue, profit, investment), each head accountable
  • Management audit — systematic appraisal of management's overall performance; finds deficiencies, keeps mgmt updated, improves control & coordination
  • PERT & CPM — network techniques to plan, schedule & control time-bound projects; critical path = longest path
  • Management Information System — computer-based system giving managers timely info for decisions & control

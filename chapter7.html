1Concept

Directing = process of instructing, guiding, counselling, motivating & leading people to achieve organisational objectives. Not just communication: it includes supervision, motivation & leadership. Done by every manager, throughout the life of the organisation.
🧠 I Got Cold Mango Lassi → Instruct · Guide · Counsel · Motivate · Lead

Elements (4)

🧠 Some Men Love Cricket
👀 SupervisionOverseeing & guiding subordinates' work
🔥 MotivationStimulating people to act willingly
🧭 LeadershipInfluencing people to strive voluntarily
💬 CommunicationExchanging ideas to create understanding

Features (4)

🧠 I Eat Cake Today
  • Initiates action — other functions prepare the setting; directing starts the action
  • Every level of management — wherever a superior–subordinate relationship exists
  • Continuous — goes on even when managers change (Infosys, Tata, BHEL)
  • Top to bottom — flows down the hierarchy; direct immediate subordinate, take orders from immediate boss

2Importance & Principles

Importance (5)

🧠 I Integrate People's Changes Smoothly
  • Initiates action — towards desired goals (supervisor clears doubts → targets met)
  • Integrates efforts — every individual effort adds to organisational performance
  • Potential realised — motivation & leadership bring out full capability
  • Change facilitated — reduces resistance via motivation, communication, leadership
  • Stability & balance — cooperation & commitment among groups & departments
📌 New accounting system resisted → explain purpose + train + reward → staff accept the change

Principles (8)

🧠 My Happy Uncle Always Makes Ugly Little Faces
  • Maximum individual contribution — bring out untapped energies (monetary + non-monetary rewards)
  • Harmony of objectives — convince staff that their rewards & work efficiency go together
  • Unity of command — orders from ONE superior only; otherwise confusion & conflict
  • Appropriateness of direction technique — suit the person (money motivates some, promotion others)
  • Managerial communication — clear instructions + feedback to check understanding
  • Use of informal organisation — spot informal groups & use them
  • Leadership — influence positively without causing dissatisfaction
  • Follow through — giving an order isn't enough; review & modify if needed

3Supervision

① As an element of directingDone by EVERY manager: overseeing subordinates & giving instructions → optimum use of resources + targets met
② As a positionSupervisor at operative level, just above workers; the only manager in direct touch with them

Importance (7)

🧠 Daily Life Gets People To Love Feedback
  • Day-to-day contact — friendly relations; guide, friend & philosopher to workers
  • Link between workers & management — passes ideas down, problems up → fewer conflicts
  • Group unity — sorts out internal differences, keeps harmony
  • Performance as per targets — takes responsibility for task achievement
  • Training (on-the-job) — builds an efficient team
  • Leadership — builds high morale among workers
  • Feedback — analyses work, suggests ways to improve skills

4Motivation

Motivation = process of stimulating people to action to accomplish desired goals. William G. Scott. Literally: incitement or inducement to act or move.
💭 MotiveInner state that energises & directs behaviour; arises from needs (hunger, security, recognition)
⚡ MotivationThe process of stimulating action; depends on satisfying needs
🎁 MotivatorThe technique used: pay, bonus, promotion, recognition, praise, responsibility
🔑 Motive = inner need · Motivation = process · Motivator = tool

Features (4)

🧠 I Got Pretty Cold
  • Internal feeling — urges, drives, desires (wanting a bike, a house)
  • Goal-directed behaviour — e.g. promotion offered → employee improves performance
  • Positive or negative — Positive: pay rise, promotion, recognition · Negative: punishment, stopping increments, threats
  • Complex — people differ, so the same motivator won't work equally on everyone

Process (6)

🧠 Ugly Toads Dance Slowly, Sing Rarely
1. Unsatisfied need
2. Tension
3. Drives
4. Search behaviour
5. Satisfied need
6. Reduction of tension
📌 Ramu skipped breakfast → restless (tension) → walks 2 km looking for a hotel (search) → ₹10 meal (satisfied) → energy back (tension reduced)

Importance (5)

🧠 Please Allow The Absent Child
  • Performance ↑ — satisfied employees give maximum effort
  • Attitude negative → positive — through rewards, praise & encouragement
  • Turnover ↓ — saves recruitment & training costs; retains talent
  • Absenteeism ↓ — work becomes a source of pleasure
  • Change accepted smoothly — if staff see extra rewards in it
📌 Workers claim inability for new work, unwilling; sudden rise in demand → Motivation

5Maslow's Need Hierarchy

Abraham Maslow (1943): every person has 5 needs in a hierarchy; once a lower need is satisfied, the next higher one motivates.
🧠 Bottom → Top: Please Stop Asking Every Student
5. Self-actualisationBecoming what one is capable of: growth, self-fulfilment, achieving goals
4. EsteemSelf-respect, autonomy, status, recognition, attention
3. Affiliation / BelongingAffection, belongingness, acceptance, friendship
2. Safety / SecurityProtection from physical & emotional harm
1. Basic PhysiologicalHunger, thirst, shelter, sleep

Examples

Need👤 Individual🏢 Organisation
Self-actualisationSelf-fulfilmentAchievement of goals
EsteemStatusJob title
AffiliationFriendshipCordial relations with colleagues
SafetyStability of incomePension plan, job security
PhysiologicalHungerBasic salary

Assumptions (4)

🧠 Big Hills Slow Sherpas
  • Behaviour based on needs — satisfying needs influences behaviour
  • Hierarchical order — from basic needs up to higher needs
  • Satisfied need stops motivating — only the next higher need motivates
  • Step up only when lower need is satisfied
⚖️ Criticism → classification & order of needs questioned · Still relevant → manager must identify an employee's need level to motivate them

6Incentives

Incentive = any measure used to motivate people to improve performance.

💰 Financial (7)

In direct money form or measurable in money; individual or group basis.
🧠 Pretty Parrots Bring Peanuts, Cashews, Raisins, Pistachios
  • Pay & allowances — basic pay + DA + allowances; yearly increments
  • Productivity-linked wage incentives — wages tied to productivity (individual/group)
  • Bonus — over and above wages/salary
  • Profit sharing — share in the organisation's profits
  • Co-partnership / Stock option — shares below market price → sense of ownership (Infosys)
  • Retirement benefits — provident fund, pension, gratuity
  • Perquisites — car allowance, housing, medical aid, children's education

🌟 Non-Financial (8)

Satisfy psychological, social & emotional needs. Money may be involved, but it isn't the main point.
🧠 Some Old Cats Jump Every Jolly Evening Early
  • Status — ranking of positions; satisfies esteem & social needs
  • Organisational climate — autonomy, reward orientation, consideration, risk-taking
  • Career advancement — skill development + sound promotion policy ("works as a tonic")
  • Job enrichment — more variety, skill, autonomy & responsibility → job itself motivates
  • Employee recognition — congratulating, notice board, awards, mementos, rewarding suggestions
  • Job security — stable income & work ✗ can make people complacent
  • Employee participation — joint management, work & canteen committees
  • Employee empowerment — more autonomy & powers → job feels important
🔑 Promotion = non-financial (status, authority & challenge outweigh the extra money)

7Leadership

Leadership = process of influencing people's behaviour so they strive voluntarily towards organisational goals. Koontz & Weihrich: "Art or process of influencing people so that they will strive willingly and enthusiastically towards the achievement of group goals."

Features (5)

🧠 I Can't Imagine Cold Coffee
  • Influence — ability to influence others
  • Change in behaviour — tries to change how others behave
  • Interpersonal relations — between leader & followers
  • Common goals — exercised for organisation's goals
  • Continuous process
🤝 Leader–follower → followers make a leader by accepting them; their skills & commitment matter too
🔑 Stephen Covey: managers are important, but leaders are vital for lasting success

Importance (5)

🧠 In Paris Children Can Travel
  • Influences behaviour — people contribute energies positively
  • Personal relations — confidence, support → congenial work environment
  • Change introduced — persuades, clarifies, inspires → minimum discontent
  • Conflict handled — lets followers vent, then persuades
  • Training successors — smooth succession
⭐ Qualities (from book summary)Courage, will power, judgement, knowledge, integrity, physical energy, faith, fairness, decisiveness, social skills. No one has them all; they can be built through training.

Styles (based on use of authority)

🧠 All Dogs Laugh → Autocratic · Democratic · Laissez-faire

🗳️ DemocraticDecides in consultation; encourages participation; uses group forces
🕊️ Laissez-faire (Free-rein)Uses power only if absolutely essential; group sets own goals & solves issues; leader only supports & informs

📌 Inflexible manager, hates contradiction; staff stressed & fearful → Autocratic style (problem)
📌 "Doesn't use power unless absolutely essential" → Laissez-faire

8Communication

Communication = process of exchanging ideas, views, facts & feelings between people to create common understanding. From Latin "communis" = common. Koontz & Weihrich: "Transfer of information from the sender to the receiver with the information being understood by the receiver."

Process Elements (8)

🧠 Some Monkeys Eat Mangoes During Rainy Fridays, Noisily
1. Sender
Source; conveys the idea
2. Message
Content: ideas, feelings, orders
3. Encoding
Converting message into words, pictures, gestures
4. Media
Path: written, face to face, phone, internet
5. Decoding
Converting symbols back into meaning
6. Receiver
Person who gets the message
7. Feedback
Receiver's response showing it was understood
8. Noise
Any obstruction, at any stage
🔊 Noise examples → ambiguous symbols · poor phone line · inattentive receiver · faulty decoding · prejudices · distorting gestures

Importance (7)

🧠 Cool Students Don't Make Childish Little Mistakes
  • Coordination basis — explains goals & inter-relationships
  • Smooth working — when communication stops, organised activity ceases
  • Decision-making basis — provides needed information
  • Managerial efficiency ↑ — conveying goals, instructions, allocating jobs
  • Cooperation & industrial peace — two-way communication builds understanding
  • Leadership — basis of influencing subordinates
  • Morale & motivation ↑ — basis of participative management
🔑 Managers spend ~90% of their time communicating · Chester Barnard: "foundation of all group activities"

9Formal vs Informal Communication

📋 Formal

Flows through official channels in the organisation chart; oral or written, usually recorded & filed.
⬆️ Vertical: UpwardSubordinate → superior: leave application, progress report, request for grants
⬇️ Vertical: DownwardSuperior → subordinate: meeting notice, work orders, guidelines
↔️ Horizontal / LateralBetween divisions: production manager ↔ marketing manager on delivery, design

Formal Networks (5)

🧠 Cats Will Chase Fat Insects
NetworkHow it works
Single chainEach superior → his subordinate, level by level
WheelAll talk via one superior (hub); subordinates can't talk to each other
CircularEach talks to 2 neighbours; SLOW
Free flowAnyone talks to anyone; FAST
Inverted VTalks to immediate boss + boss's boss (only prescribed matters with the latter)

☕ Informal (Grapevine)

Communication outside formal lines; spreads in all directions regardless of authority levels. Arises from employees' need to share views that formal channels can't carry.
📌 Canteen chit-chat about the boss · rumours of transfers
❌ NegativesGets distorted, source hard to trace, rumours, may hamper work environment
✅ PositivesVery fast; managers use it to test subordinates' reactions
🔑 Smart manager → use its positives, minimise its negatives

Grapevine Networks (4)

🧠 Silly Girls Play Cards
  • Single strand — each tells the next, in sequence
  • Gossip — one tells everyone, non-selectively
  • Probability — tells others at random
  • Cluster — tells only trusted people → MOST POPULAR

10Barriers & Improvement

🧠 4 types: Some People Over Panic → Semantic · Psychological · Organisational · Personal

🔤 Semantic (6): encoding/decoding problems

🧠 Bad Spelling Feels Unusually Tough, Bro
  • Badly expressed message — poor vocabulary, wrong or missing words
  • Symbols with different meanings — "value" of a ring / friendship / skills
  • Faulty translations — English → Hindi by a non-proficient translator
  • Unclarified assumptions — "Take care of our guest" (full stay vs just drop to hotel)
  • Technical jargon — specialists confuse non-specialists
  • Body language mismatch — words say one thing, gestures another

🧠 Psychological (4): state of mind

🧠 Polite Lions Lick Deer
  • Premature evaluation — judging before the sender finishes (prejudice)
  • Lack of attention — boss busy with a file ignores employee
  • Loss by transmission & poor retention — passing through levels distorts (esp. oral); people forget
  • Distrust — parties don't believe each other

🏢 Organisational (5): structure & rules

🧠 Proper Rules Stop Complex Failures
  • Policy — not supportive of free flow (highly centralised)
  • Rules & regulations — rigid procedures, prescribed channels → delays
  • Status — psychological distance between superior & subordinates
  • Complex structure — many levels → more filtering points → delay & distortion
  • Facilities lacking — meetings, suggestion/complaint boxes, gatherings, transparency

👤 Personal (4): sender & receiver

🧠 Five Little Unicorns Laughed
  • Fear of challenge to authority — superior withholds communication
  • Lack of confidence in subordinates — superior doesn't seek their opinion
  • Unwillingness to communicate — subordinate fears it hurts his interests
  • Lack of proper incentives — no reward for suggestions → none offered

✅ Improving Communication (9)

🧠 Clever Newbies Can Learn Very Fast; Practice Follows Listening
  • Clarify ideas before communicating — study & analyse the problem first
  • Needs of receiver — match their education & understanding level
  • Consult others — involvement → ready acceptance & cooperation
  • Language, tone & content — understandable, not offensive, stimulating
  • Value to listeners — link message to their interests & needs
  • Feedback ensured — ask questions, encourage responses
  • Present & future — meet current commitments + aim at future goals
  • Follow up — review instructions, remove hurdles
  • Listen well — patient listening solves half the problems

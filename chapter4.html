1Concept

Planning = deciding in advance what to do & how to do it. Full definition: setting objectives for a given time period, formulating various courses of action to achieve them, and selecting the best alternative.
🧠 Main aspects: Smart Teens Find Solutions → Set objectives · Time period · Formulate alternatives · Select the best
🎯 Ends & MeansEnds = what is to be done · Means = how it is to be done
🌉 BridgeFills the gap between where we are & where we want to go
⏳ Time frameTime is limited; if ignored, conditions change & plans go waste
🚀 Must be acted uponA plan that isn't implemented is a futile exercise
💡 AlsoBasic managerial function · done at all levels · linked to creativity & innovation · needs decisions (choice among alternatives)
📌 IOCL: ₹20,000 cr planned for 2017-18 expansion & overseas markets (Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar) → broad plans must be broken into steps for implementation

2Importance (6)

🧠 D-R-O-I-D-S → DROIDS
  • Directions provided — clear goals guide action; departments work in coordination, not in different directions
  • Reduces risks of uncertainty — anticipates changes & prepares responses (changes can't be eliminated)
  • Overlapping & wasteful activities reduced — clarity in thought & action; useless activities removed; inefficiencies easier to detect
  • Innovative ideas promoted — first function, so new ideas take shape as concrete plans
  • Decision making facilitated — evaluate alternatives, select the most viable → rational decisions
  • Standards for controlling — goals = standards to measure actual performance against; correct deviations
🔑 Planning is a prerequisite for controlling → no goals or standards = no deviations to find

3Features (7)

🧠 Only People Plan Carefully For Daily Meals
  • Objectives focused — purposeful; meaningless unless it helps achieve goals
  • Primary function — base for all other functions; precedes them = "primacy of planning"
  • Pervasive — all levels & departments; Top: whole org · Middle: departmental · Supervisors: day-to-day
  • Continuous — planning cycle: plan framed → implemented → new plan for next period
  • Futuristic — looks ahead, based on forecasting (sales forecast → annual production & sales plan)
  • Decision making — needs alternatives; only one option = no need to plan
  • Mental exercise — foresight, intelligent imagination, sound judgement; thinking, not doing; logical, not guesswork
📌 Polaris sets expansion target first (800 more professionals in 6 months) → objectives come first = primary function
📌 C Ltd: team from all levels brainstorms new models to improve future market standing → Pervasive · Futuristic · Mental exercise

4Limitations (6)

🧠 Rich Dads Can't Count Their Gold
  • Rigidity — fixed goals & time frames; managers can't adapt when circumstances change
  • Dynamic environment — policy changes, political instability, natural calamity, competition → targets & cash budgets revised
  • Creativity reduced — top plans, others only implement; can't deviate → initiative lost
  • Costs huge — time & money; board meetings, experts, viability studies; may not justify benefits
  • Time-consuming — little time left for implementation
  • Guarantee of success absent — relying on past successful plans → complacency & false sense of security
🔑 Despite limits → not useless; a tool to use with caution; not a solution to all problems

⚖️ "Planning reduces creativity": critically comment

✅ Promotes innovationFirst function → new ideas take shape as concrete plans; guides growth
❌ Reduces creativityDone by top mgmt; others just follow orders, can't deviate
📌 Why can't organisations achieve all objectives? / Why no guaranteed success? → answer with the limitations above (dynamic environment, rigidity, complacency, poor implementation)
📌 Can planning work in a changing environment? → Not fully: it can't foresee everything, so plans need flexibility & revision

5Planning Process (7 Steps)

🧠 Smart Dogs In Egypt Sniff Interesting Food
1. Setting objectives
For org & each dept (e.g. sales ↑ 20%); percolate down to all levels; managers participate
2. Developing premises
Premises = assumptions about the future (forecasts, existing plans, past policies); same for all, total agreement
3. Identifying alternatives
List all possible courses: routine or innovative; important project → more alternatives
4. Evaluating alternatives
Weigh pros & cons vs objective (risk-return trade-off); feasibility & consequences
5. Selecting an alternative
Real point of decision making; most feasible, profitable, least negative; may combine plans; judgement & intuition
6. Implementing the plan
Putting it into action; other functions come in (more output → organise labour, buy machinery)
7. Follow-up action
Monitor whether activities run to schedule → objectives achieved
🔮 Forecasts used for premises → demand, policy change, interest rates, capital goods prices, tax rates
📌 Ms Rajni lists options (new markets, wider range, discounts, more ads) to raise share 10% → 25% → Identifying alternative courses of action

7Types of Plans (8)

🧠 Old Sailors Prefer Pirate Maps, Rum, Parrots & Boats

🎯 1. Objectives

Desired future position management wants to reach = the end result of activities.
  • Nature — end point of planning; all activities directed towards them; e.g. sales ↑ 10%, 20% profit
  • Set by — top management; broad, general issues; depts may set their own
  • Must be — measurable (quantitative), written, with a time period

♟️ 2. Strategy

Comprehensive plan for accomplishing objectives; defines the organisation's direction & scope in the long run.
🧠 3 dimensions: Lions Chase Antelopes → Long-term objectives · Course of action · Allocating resources
  • Considers — business environment (economic, political, social, legal, technological) & competition
  • Strategic decisions — stay in same line? add new lines? seek dominant position in the market?
  • Marketing strategy asks — who are customers? demand? distribution channel? pricing? advertising?

🧭 3. Policy

General statements that guide thinking or channelise energies in a particular direction.
  • Role — interprets strategy; general response to a problem; manager uses discretion
  • Major vs minor — Major: for all to know (customers, competitors) · Minor: insiders, minute details
  • Purchase policy — make or buy? how to select vendors? how many suppliers? criteria?

📋 4. Procedure

Routine steps on how to carry out activities, in chronological order.
  • Nature — exact manner of work; for insiders; enforces a policy; works within the policy framework
  • Example — requisitioning supplies before production; reporting progress in production

🛠️ 5. Method

Prescribed manner in which a task (ONE step of a procedure) is to be performed.
  • Nature — varies task to task; right method saves time, money, effort → efficiency
  • Example — training: lectures & seminars for top level, on-the-job for supervisors

🚫 6. Rule

Specific statement of what must or must not be done; no flexibility or discretion.
  • Nature — simplest plan; no compromise; changes only by a policy decision
  • Why a plan? — decided in advance & guides action = deciding in advance what to do

📦 7. Programme

Detailed statement about a project: objectives, policies, procedures, rules, tasks, human & physical resources, and budget.
ScopeEntire gamut of activities; minutest details within the policy framework
vs ProjectSimilar, but differ in scope & complexity

💰 8. Budget

Statement of expected results in numerical terms; quantifies future facts & figures.
  • Examples — sales budget (sales per product per area per month); workers needed at peak production
  • Also a control device — compare actual vs expected figures, then correct (but involves forecasting → it's a plan)
  • Cash budget — Inflows − Outflows = Surplus / Deficiency; avoid excess cash (little or no return)
🔑 Chain: Strategy → Policy → Procedure → Method (policy interprets strategy · procedure enforces policy · method = one step of a procedure)

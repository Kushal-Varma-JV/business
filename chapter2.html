Evolution of Management Thought (6 phases)

2Nature (7)

🧠 Ugly Goats Find Fresh Bananas & Crunchy Carrots
  • Universal applicability — all orgs: business/non-business, small/large, public/private, manufacturing/services; extent varies (division of work in a govt office AND a company)
  • General guidelines — no readymade, straitjacket solutions (dept conflict → stress overall org goals)
  • Formed by practice & experimentation — experience + collective wisdom (discipline) + experiments (fatigue: improve physical conditions)
  • Flexible — modified as situation demands; like tools for different purposes (degree of centralisation)
  • Behavioural (mainly) — aim to influence human behaviour; also link human & material resources (factory layout)
  • Cause & effect — predict likely effect; less than perfect (humans). Emergency → one takes charge; new factory → participative
  • Contingent — depend on the situation (fair pay depends on contribution, paying capacity, prevailing wage rate)
🔑 Derivation of principles = Science · Creative application = Art → makes management learnable & teachable (post by qualification, not birth)
📌 Innovation at Toyota & Biocon (Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw) → principles are flexible general guidelines, applied creatively

3Significance (6)

🧠 I Only Sing Melodies So Tenderly
  • Insights into reality — more knowledge; learn from past mistakes; solve recurring problems fast → efficiency (delegate routine decisions)
  • Optimum utilisation of resources & effective administration — max benefit, min cost; no trial-and-error waste; limits managerial discretion → no personal bias (budgets based on contribution to org goals)
  • Scientific decisions — based on facts & logic, not blind faith; timely, realistic, measurable, unbiased
  • Meeting changing environment — principles get modified (division of work → core competency: HLL sold chemicals & seeds; outsourcing, BPO, KPO)
  • Social responsibility — "equity" now covers customers, environment, business associates (BHEL township Ranipur; Lijjat Papad)
  • Training, education & research — basis of MBA/BBA & aptitude tests; led to OR, cost accounting, JIT, Kanban, Kaizen
📝 Book summary splits these into 8 → "optimum utilisation" + "effective administration" listed separately, and "training" + "research" listed separately

4Taylor's Scientific Management

👤 F.W. TaylorAmerican mechanical engineer (1856–1915)
👑 TitleFather of Scientific Management
📖 Work"The Principles of Scientific Management" (1911); coined the term
🏭 ResultBethlehem Steel: 3× productivity
Scientific management = conducting business using standardised tools, methods & trained personnel to ↑ output, ↑ quality, ↓ costs & wastes. Taylor: "Knowing exactly what you want men to do and seeing that they do it in the best and cheapest way."
👍 Rule of ThumbPersonal judgment; trial & error; differs manager to manager
🔬 Scientific methodDefine problem → alternatives → anticipate consequences → measure → conclude

Principles (5)

🧠 Smart Heroes Can Make Differences
  • Science, not rule of thumb — find ONE best method by work-study → standardise & use it everywhere (even loading pig iron can be planned)
  • Harmony, not discord — no class conflict between managers & workers; mental revolution; management shares gains, workers work hard & accept change (Japanese strikers wear black badges but work extra)
  • Cooperation, not individualism — extension of harmony; competition → cooperation; reward workers' suggestions, consult them; workers avoid strikes & unreasonable demands; almost equal division of work & responsibility
  • Maximum, not restricted output (book summary) — both sides work to maximise output & enlarge the surplus
  • Development of each person to greatest efficiency & prosperity — scientific selection → work suited to abilities → training → produce more, earn more

🧠 Mental Revolution

Change in attitude of workers & management towards each other, from competition to cooperation. The "sum and substance" of Taylor's ideas.
🔑 Grow the surplus instead of fighting over it → management shares surplus · workers give their best · no need for agitation
📌 Principle that extends "Harmony, not discord" → Cooperation, not individualism

📏 Standardisation & Simplification

StandardisationSetting standards (benchmarks) for every activity: process, raw material, time, product, machinery, methods, working conditions
SimplificationEliminating unnecessary varieties, sizes & dimensions → saves labour, machine & tool costs; lower inventory; fuller use of equipment; turnover ↑
🎯 Objectives of standardisation (4): Fat Iguanas Quietly Pose
  • Fixed types — reduce a line/product to fixed types, sizes & characteristics
  • Interchangeability — of manufactured parts & products
  • Quality standards — excellence & quality in materials
  • Performance standards — of men & machines
📌 Nokia, Toyota, Microsoft → large market share via standardisation & simplification

6Work Study & Wages

🧠 Work study (4): My Mom Takes Five → Method · Motion · Time · Fatigue
🛤️ Method StudyOne best way of doing the job, from raw material to delivery; min cost, max quality & customer satisfaction. Led to the assembly line (Ford). Tools: process charts, OR
🤸 Motion StudyStudy movements (lifting, sitting, changing position); remove unnecessary ones. Motions = productive · incidental (going to stores) · unproductive. Bricklaying 18 → 5 motions (Gilbreth), ~4× productivity
⏱️ Time StudyStandard time for a well-defined job (several readings). Aim: number of workers, incentive schemes, labour cost
😴 Fatigue StudyAmount & frequency of rest intervals → regain stamina → productivity ↑
🔑 Method = which way · Motion = which moves · Time = how long · Fatigue = how much rest
📌 Time study: 1 box in 20 min → 3/hour × 7 hours (8 minus 1 hour break) = 21 boxes = standard task
😩 Causes of fatigue → long hours · unsuitable work · bad relations with boss · poor working conditions

💰 Differential Piece Wage System

Different wage rates for workers who meet/beat the standard vs those below it, to reward efficiency. Standards come from work study. Taylor's tool for telling efficient workers from inefficient ones.
✅ Efficient (≥10 units @ ₹50)11 × 50 = ₹550
❌ Inefficient (<10 units @ ₹40)9 × 40 = ₹360
🔑 Difference ₹190 → motivates the inefficient worker to improve
📌 Schmidt (Bethlehem) → pig iron 12.5 → 47 tons/day; wages $1.15 → $1.85 (+60%); labourers needed 500 → 140

8Fayol's 14 Principles

🧠 Dad And Daughter Usually Use Some Red Crayons; Sister Only Eats Sweet Ice-cream Everyday
  • 1. Division of work — small tasks, each by a trained specialist → specialisation; "more and better work for the same effort"; applies to technical & managerial work (separate finance, marketing, production, HR depts)
  • 2. Authority & responsibility — authority = right to give orders & get obedience; responsibility = its corollary; must be balanced; safeguards against misuse; punish only after a hearing. Authority: official (position) + personal (individual)
  • 3. Discipline — obey org rules & employment agreement; needs good superiors, clear & fair agreements, judicious penalties (workers do unpaid overtime, management raises wages later: both honour the deal)
  • 4. Unity of command — one boss per employee; no dual subordination. If violated: "authority undermined, discipline in jeopardy, order disturbed, stability threatened"
  • 5. Unity of direction — one head + one plan for each group of activities with the same objective → unity of action & coordination
  • 6. Subordination of individual interest to general interest — org's interest comes before any one person's; manager sets an example (no misuse of power for family benefit)
  • 7. Remuneration of employees — fair to both: reasonable standard of living for staff, within company's paying capacity → just & equitable → good relations
  • 8. Centralisation & decentralisation — concentration vs dispersal of decision-making authority; balance needed; large orgs more decentralised (panchayats get more powers)
  • 9. Scalar chain — formal line of authority & communication from top to bottom; must be followed; gang plank allowed in emergencies
  • 10. Order — "A place for everything (everyone) and everything (everyone) in its place" → no hindrance → productivity ↑
  • 11. Equity — kindliness & justice; no discrimination (sex, religion, language, caste, belief, nationality); lazy staff dealt with sternly → loyalty & devotion (MNCs)
  • 12. Stability of personnel — minimise turnover; rigorous selection, then a minimum fixed tenure to show results; otherwise recruitment, selection & training costs rise
  • 13. Initiative — encourage workers to make & carry out improvement plans (not defying established practices); reward suggestions via an employee suggestion system
  • 14. Esprit de corps — team spirit, unity & harmony; replace "I" with "We" → mutual trust & belongingness; fewer penalties needed

⚖️ Unity of Command vs Unity of Direction

BasisUnity of CommandUnity of Direction
MeaningOne subordinate receives orders from & reports to only one superiorEach group of activities with the same objective has one head & one plan
AimPrevents dual subordinationPrevents overlapping of activities
ImplicationsAffects an individual employeeAffects the entire organisation
✅ Unity of command useful because → no confusion over tasks, clear accountability, discipline & stability maintained

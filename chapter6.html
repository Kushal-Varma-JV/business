1Concept

Staffing = managerial function of filling & keeping filled the positions in the organisation structure. Simplest terms: "putting people to jobs". Concerned with obtaining, utilising & maintaining a satisfactory and satisfied workforce.
🆕 New enterpriseFollows planning & organising (structure reveals HR needs)
🔁 Existing enterpriseContinuous: new jobs created, employees leave
📋 CoversWorkforce planning → recruitment, selection, placement, training, development, promotion, compensation, appraisal (incl. daily wagers, consultants, contract staff)
🔑 Aim = Right person, right job, in the right number, at the right time
📌 Infosys: "Our assets walk out of the door each evening" → people = most important asset; must attract, retain & develop them

2Importance (5)

🧠 Don't Hire Sleepy Old Joggers
  • Discovers competent personnel — finds & obtains capable people for various jobs
  • Higher performance — right person on the right job
  • Survival & growth — through succession planning for managers
  • Optimum utilisation — avoids overmanning (under-use, high labour cost) & flags shortages in advance
  • Job satisfaction & morale — objective assessment & fair reward
📈 More important today due to → rapid technology change · bigger organisations · complicated human behaviour
⚠️ Wrong people → waste of material, time & effort → low productivity, poor quality, lower profits

3Staffing & HRM

Staffing = inherent part of HRM: finding, evaluating & establishing a working relationship with people.
👔 Generic functionEvery manager performs it; deals with the human element
🏢 Small vs large orgSmall: manager does all HR duties · Large: separate HR department of specialists
↕️ Line + Staff activityLine = essential function of every manager · Staff = advisory role of the HR department
🔀 BothA function of management (like planning, directing) AND a distinct functional area (like marketing, finance)

HRM Duties (8)

🧠 Really Angry Cats Trash Little Gardens So Deliberately
  • Recruitment — search for qualified people
  • Analysing jobs — collect info → prepare job descriptions
  • Compensation — develop pay & incentive plans
  • Training & development — efficient performance + career growth
  • Labour relations — maintain union–management relations
  • Grievances — handle complaints
  • Social security — welfare of employees
  • Defending lawsuits — avoid legal complications

Evolution of HRM

1. Labour Welfare Officer
Trade union movement needed a link between owners & workers; minimal welfare; looked down on by both
2. Personnel Officer → Manager
Factory system: thousands under one roof → recruitment, selection & placement
3. HR Manager
Human relations approach + fast-changing technology → people seen as a resource to develop
🔑 Welfare → Personnel → Human Resource

5Recruitment

Recruitment = process of searching for prospective employees & stimulating them to apply for jobs in the organisation. Comes before selection. Objective: create a pool of suitable candidates.
🧠 Activities: I Ate Cold Ice-cream → Identify sources · Assess validity · Choose best source · Invite applications
Sources of Recruitment
🏠 Internal (2)🌍 External (10)

🏠 Internal Sources

↔️ TransferHorizontal shift (job / dept / shift); no substantive change in responsibility & status; salary not necessarily changed
⬆️ PromotionVertical shift to a higher position: more responsibility, facilities, status & pay
Transfer also → fills shortages from overstaffed depts · avoids termination · resolves grievances · trains employees
✅ My Sister Treats Strangers Cheaply (5)
  • Motivation — chain of promotions → commitment, loyalty, peace
  • Simplifies selection — candidates already known → accurate & reliable evaluation
  • Training tool — transfers prepare for higher jobs; no induction training needed
  • Shifts surplus staff — from overstaffed depts to short-staffed ones
  • Cheaper — than external sources
❌ I Let Nobody Cheat Frequently (5)
  • Inbreeding — no fresh talent ("new blood")
  • Lethargy — if time-bound promotions are assured
  • New enterprise can't use it — and no firm can fill ALL vacancies internally
  • Competition hampered — among employees
  • Frequent transfers — reduce productivity

🌍 External Sources (10)

🧠 Do Cats Always Eat Pizza? Can Rabbits Like Tasty Waffles?
  • Direct recruitment — notice on board, on-the-spot selection; casual/"badli" workers on daily wages; cheapest; for rush of work or absent workers
  • Casual callers — database of unsolicited applicants; reduces cost
  • Advertisement — newspapers/journals; wider choice, more info, senior posts ✗ flood of unsuitable applicants
  • Employment exchange — govt-run; unskilled & skilled operative jobs; sometimes legally compulsory ✗ outdated records
  • Placement agencies & mgmt consultants — charge a fee; extensive screening; lure top executives; consultants focus on middle & top level
  • Campus recruitment — colleges & institutes; technical, professional, managerial jobs
  • Recommendations of employees — background known; preliminary screening already done (Infosys, Lenovo referrals)
  • Labour contractors — unskilled workers at short notice ✗ if the contractor leaves, his workers leave too
  • Television — job details + organisation profile telecast
  • Web publishing — job websites used by both job seekers & employers
✅ Quick Wins Feel Cool (4)
  • Qualified personnel — attracts qualified & trained people
  • Wider choice — large number of applicants
  • Fresh talent — brings new blood
  • Competitive spirit — existing staff work harder to compete with outsiders
❌ Don't Lose Cash (3)
  • Dissatisfaction — existing staff feel promotion chances are reduced
  • Lengthy — notify vacancies, wait for applications
  • Costly — advertising & processing applications
📌 Paper plate firm, urgent festival order of 50,000 extra → Direct recruitment (casual workers)
📌 Kaul Consultants' naukaripao.com for senior jobs → Web publishing
📌 Xylo Ltd setting up a new plant → External sources (a new firm can't use internal)
📌 Why is internal more economical? → no advertising cost · candidates already known · no induction training

6Selection

Selection = process of identifying & choosing the best person out of a number of prospective candidates for a job. Many are eliminated at every stage. Its effectiveness is finally tested by the chosen person's on-the-job performance.
🏆 Purpose 1Org gets the best among those available
🌟 Purpose 2Raises self-esteem & prestige of those selected; shows the org's seriousness

Process (8)

🧠 Puppies Sit, Eat, Run, Sleep, Munch, Jump, Cuddle
1. Preliminary screening
Reject unfit applicants using application forms + preliminary interview
2. Selection tests
Measure aptitude, intelligence, personality (see below)
3. Employment interview
Formal, in-depth conversation to judge suitability (two-way today)
4. Reference & background checks
Verify info via previous employers, teachers, professors
5. Selection decision
Concerned manager's view counts most (responsible for performance)
6. Medical examination
After selection decision, BEFORE job offer
7. Job offer
Letter of appointment with reporting date; reasonable time given
8. Contract of employment
Attestation form + contract (title, duties, pay, hours, leave, grievance, termination)
❌ Fail any of steps 1 to 5 → application rejected

Selection Tests (5)

🧠 I Ate Pizza Then Ice-cream
  • Intelligence — IQ level; learning ability, decision-making & judgment
  • Aptitude — potential to learn new skills; predicts future success
  • Personality — emotions, reactions, maturity, values; difficult to design
  • Trade — existing skills; knowledge & proficiency in the profession
  • Interest — pattern of interests in a job
Aptitude testPOTENTIAL to acquire skills (future)
Trade testACTUAL skills possessed (present)
🔑 Aptitude = "can learn" · Trade = "can do"

4 Possible Outcomes

✅ Correct acceptPredicted to succeed → succeeds
✅ Correct rejectPredicted to fail → would have failed
❌ Reject errorRejected someone who would have succeeded
❌ Accept errorAccepted someone who performs poorly
📌 Security firm needs reliable staff who won't leak secrets → Reference & background checks + personality test

7Training & Development

T&D = attempt to improve current or future performance by increasing an employee's ability through learning (changing attitude or increasing skills & knowledge). "If you wish to plan for a lifetime, develop people."
📈 Why needed now → jobs more complex + rapid technological change

Benefits to Organisation (5)

🧠 Some Pilots Fly More Routes
  • Systematic learning — better than trial & error (less waste of effort & money)
  • Productivity ↑ — quantity & quality → higher profits
  • Future managers — ready to take over in an emergency
  • Morale ↑ — absenteeism & employee turnover ↓
  • Response to change — technological & economic

Benefits to Employee (4)

🧠 Cats Eat All Sardines
  • Career — better skills & knowledge → better career
  • Earnings — higher performance → earns more
  • Accidents ↓ — handles machines efficiently
  • Satisfaction & morale ↑

Training · Education · Development

🛠️ TrainingIncreases skills & abilities for a SPECIFIC job; tied to org goals; short-term
📚 EducationIncreases knowledge & understanding; builds a logical, rational mind; broader than training
🌱 DevelopmentAll-round growth of personality & potential; ongoing; includes training → "better men & women"
🔑 Training = job · Education = mind · Development = whole person

8Training Methods

🏭 On-the-jobAt the workplace while working = learning WHILE doing
🏫 Off-the-jobAway from the workplace = learning BEFORE doing

On-the-job (4)

🧠 A Cool Indian Job
  • Apprenticeship — under a master worker; higher skills (plumbers, electricians); uniform period for fast & slow learners
  • Coaching — superior sets mutual goals, reviews progress; trainee groomed to replace the senior manager
  • Internship — joint programme of educational institutes + firms; study + practical work
  • Job rotation — shifted across depts/jobs; broad understanding; tests aptitude; eases promotions & transfers

Off-the-job (6)

🧠 Cool Friends Can Cook Very Peacefully
  • Classroom lectures / conferences — convey specific info (rules, procedures, methods); audio-visuals help
  • Films — demonstrate skills that other methods can't show easily
  • Case study — real problems → identify, analyse causes, find alternatives, choose & implement
  • Computer modelling — computer simulates the job; learn without the risk or cost of real mistakes
  • Vestibule training — same equipment, set up away from the work floor; for sophisticated machinery
  • Programmed instruction — info broken into units, simple → complex; trainee answers questions / fills blanks
🔑 Vestibule = real equipment in a mock setup · Computer modelling = computer simulation
📌 Workers idle on hi-tech machines, engineer's frequent visits raise costs → Vestibule training
